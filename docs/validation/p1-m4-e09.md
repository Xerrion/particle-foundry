## Focused results

Commands ran from `engine/` on this Mac with pinned Rust 1.98.1:

```sh
cargo test --locked -p particle-sim-cpu water:: -- --nocapture
cargo test --locked -p particle-sim-cpu --test water_vessel

```

The five property tests and nine vessel tests passed. Clippy passed.
The property checks compare all nine published points from Tables 5, 15 and 35.
Their relative tolerance is `5e-9`, consistent with the rounded published values.
Private source-equation tests include points outside the public table.
They do not expand the runtime domain.

| Property | Maximum measured relative interpolation error | Required upper bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Saturation pressure | 4.364e-6 | 5e-6 |
| Liquid specific volume | 5.051e-7 | 2e-6 |
| Vapor specific volume | 5.178e-6 | 1e-5 |
| Liquid specific internal energy | 1.312e-7 | 2e-6 |
| Vapor specific internal energy | 1.457e-7 | 2e-6 |

Measurements cover quarter, midpoint and three-quarter positions in every
table interval. These sampled maxima are not a mathematical bound for every
temperature. Halving the spacing reduced each sampled maximum below 30 percent
of its previous value, for both refinement levels.

Closed-vessel fixtures use masses of 1e-6, 1 and 1e6 kg, five temperatures and
four partial vapor fractions. They check mass and volume sums within `2e-15`
relative error and energy residual below `1.1e-11` of input U.
Each equilibrium repeats 1000 times with the same result and unchanged inventory.
Saturated endpoints have separate tests.

A rigid vessel accepts 100 heat increments of 1000 J, followed by matching
cooling increments. The source ledger and U return to their initial values.
Heating increases temperature, pressure and vapor mass for this fixture.
Different available volumes at equal mass/U produce different equilibrium states.
This comparison does not claim moving-wall work or flow coupling.

Invalid inputs, unsupported states, excessive heat/cooling and nonfinite sources
reject. Rejected thermal edits preserve inventory and derived observations.
The heat ledger uses the representable energy change, including zero for
increments smaller than floating-point resolution.

## Bounded air/steam mixture extension

Run date: 3 October 2026. Base: Forgejo `main` at
`251e94585ff77c1a60e0602333a42b4fb8c85336`, which includes the merged single-phase
water reference at `a3e633a`. Branch: `codex/e09-air-steam-mixture`.
The primary repository is now
[Forgejo](https://git.xerrion.io/xerrion/particle-foundry).
The model version is `e09-air-steam-v1`.

`MixtureInventory` retains water mass, inert air mass, U and actual available
volume. `close_mixture` derives phase masses, temperature and total/partial
pressures. `MixtureVessel` applies external heat atomically.
The [topic article](../thermodynamics.md#airsteam-mixture-approximation) owns the
approximation, component limits and domain. No fluid, browser or GPU caller was
added. The fixed air coefficients and humidity omissions are model choices;
these results do not establish accuracy against independent humid-air data.

Commands from the root:

```sh
mise run rust:test -- -p particle-sim-cpu --lib water::mixture -- --nocapture
mise run rust:test -- -p particle-sim-cpu --test water_mixture
mise run rust:clippy
```

The 20 mixture integration tests pass. Fixtures cover two-phase water at 330,
400 and 450 K, a 600 K superheated mixture, masses scaled by 1e-6, 1 and 1e6,
and repeated closure without inventory changes. They distinguish steam partial
pressure from total pressure, use liquid properties at total pressure and verify
that liquid displacement reduces the shared air/steam volume.
Conservation assertions allow `2e-15` relative mass error, `2e-12` relative
occupied-volume error and `1.1e-11` of the energy scale for reconstructed U.
Closure tolerance is separate from property interpolation error.

Heating and cooling cross the zero-liquid boundary and return both the inventory
and derived observations to their initial values. The source ledger uses the
actual representable change in U. Rejected inputs, unsupported states and
nonfinite/excessive heat leave state unchanged.
Boundary tests include the water saturation floor, 10 kPa vapor partial pressure,
1 MPa total pressure and 623.15 K superheated vapor. Analytic dry-air tests cover
both temperature and pressure endpoints. Exactly zero air delegates to the
existing water closure, including its wider liquid-pressure domain.

Independent review reproduced `IterationLimit` for a 330 K pressure-cap state
with vapor fraction `1e-4`, and for a 330 K state with vapor fraction `1e-8`
and 100 Pa air partial pressure. The pressure-bound gas-volume endpoint already
met the occupied-volume tolerance. The corrected solve accepts that endpoint
after checking phase masses and pressure. Separate regressions cover these
fixtures, vapor fractions from `1e-8` to `1e-4` and pressure-cap states at 330,
400 and 450 K.
Further probes with vapor fraction `1e-12` and air partial pressure `1e-8 Pa`
exceeded the 64-step linear gas-volume search near the feasible-temperature
boundary. Logarithmic gas-volume bisection resolves that scale disparity within
the same budget. Regression fixtures cover these small positive carriers at
330, 400 and 450 K. They assert conservation and successful closure, without
claiming relative accuracy for negligible phase amounts or partial pressures.
Independent forward-state sweeps close all 180 probe states after these
corrections: 84 mixed states, 48 scaled dry-air endpoints and 48 scaled mixed
vapor-floor/total-pressure endpoints. Dry-air endpoint checks allow a few ulps of derived arithmetic
roundoff; scaled boundary fixtures cover 273.15, 400 and 623.15 K at both pressure
limits. Inputs remain unchanged, and queries 1 Pa outside the pressure domain
still reject.
The mixed vapor-floor matrix combines the minimum temperature with total pressure
1 MPa and the maximum temperature with nearly zero air pressure. It covers mass
scales from 1e-6 to 1e6. Its shared volume comparison uses the same subtraction
and tolerance as the existing vapor inverse, so endpoint roundoff cannot make
the feasibility bracket admit a state that its property inversion rejects.

A fixed-inventory sweep checks 12 water/air configurations and 611 temperatures
per configuration. Its 4537 feasible samples form contiguous intervals with
increasing energy and total pressure. Both partial-liquid and all-vapor states
occur. This sampled result supports the temperature bisection assumption; it
does not prove monotonicity for every possible inventory. Clippy passes.

Finite ventilation, transport/conduction, topology, ice, hot fire/water domains,
current elemental families and GPU parity remain incomplete. Inert carrier-air
support does not establish O2/product transport or FIRE-PRECONDITIONS.

### Vent acceptance fixtures

The fixtures require sealed, zero-duration and equal-pressure controls to
preserve inventory and observations exactly. Active inflow and outflow must
change pressure gradually through signed water/air mass and enthalpy flux.
The source ledger must use representable mass changes and report energy
roundoff separately. Gas composition must exclude retained liquid water.

Analytic dry-air controls distinguish enthalpy transport from internal-energy
transport. Two-phase water/air and pure-steam cases check upstream gas properties
and closure after exchange. Scaled inventories and time refinement check
conservation and explicit-timestep behavior. Invalid inputs, oversized flow,
pressure crossing and unsupported candidate states must reject without edits.

Commands from the root:

```sh
mise run rust:test -- -p particle-sim-cpu --test water_vent
mise run ci
```

The 16 vent integration tests pass. Ledger assertions compare actual component
and U changes exactly. Source enthalpy uses independent upstream property
lookups. Closure assertions retain the existing `1.1e-11` energy-scale and
`2e-12` relative occupied-volume tolerances. Absent components remain absent.
Dilute steam ingress below the supported partial-pressure floor rejects the
whole candidate, including its proposed water, air and U edits.

Dry-air inflow refinement approaches the analytic linear-resistance relaxation.
Adiabatic discharge refinement approaches `T/T0 = (m/m0)^0.4` and
`p/p0 = (m/m0)^1.4`. These independent relations distinguish upstream enthalpy
transport from internal-energy-only transport. Extensive inventory and
conductance scales of 1e-6, 1 and 1e6 retain the same derived state.

Independent CPU review approves the final change without findings. Its 288
probe combinations include 214 accepted exchanges and 74 expected rejections.
Accepted probes check actual component/U ledgers, upstream enthalpy, donor
availability, fixed volume and pressure-crossing guards. Rejected transactions
preserve the complete vessel. Dry-air exchanges pass at mass scales from
1e-150 to 1e280. An exact requested 1 percent transfer can reject when its
representable mass change exceeds the guard. The operator preserves that bound.
